# THE CURATOR'S VOICE

**2026-07-31 · drafted by CUI V24 · Rich rules · not yet accepted.**

This is derived, not invented. Every rule below was read out of copy that is
already in the product and already accepted on the glass — the r02 intake
states, the silo lines in `effect-registry.ts`, the cap modal, the collection
lines. Where a rule has an example, the example is real.

Read this with `CURATOR-STATE-INVENTORY-2026-07-31.md`, which lists every
place the studio speaks and what it says today. This document is *how*; that
one is *where* and *what*.

---

## 1 · WHO IS SPEAKING

The Curator is a person who runs a studio. Not a brand, not a system, not an
assistant. They have taste, they have limits, and they take responsibility.

Three things follow from that, and most mistakes are a failure of one:

**They say "I".** *"I'm crafting it again now."* *"I couldn't make this one
the way it deserves."* *"Let me craft these first."* Never "we", never "the
system", never the passive voice used to avoid an owner.

**They have opinions and give them.** *"That is the brave choice, and the one
people keep on the shelf."* *"My own favourite in stone."* A Curator with no
preferences is a form field with a serif.

**They do not grovel.** *"I couldn't make this one the way it deserves — so I
won't send it."* That is a failure, stated once, with the decision that
follows it. No second apology, no explaining at length, no self-flagellation.
Accountability without abasement.

---

## 2 · THE TWO REGISTERS — the most important rule here

**The letter and the receipt are different voices, and they never blend.**

The Curator writes the letter. The studio states the fact. Look at intake
state 4 as it stands:

> *I couldn't make this one the way it deserves — so I won't send it. It's
> refunded. No piece reaches you unless it's right.* — C.
>
> Refunds settle in 5–10 days, on the card you used.

First part: first person, em-dashes, signed. Second part: flat, unsigned,
sans, a plain fact about money. That separation is why the money line is
believable — it isn't being sold to you.

**Money, timing, and counts are always in the second register.** Prices,
credits, refund windows, "nothing has been charged yet". The Curator never
quotes a price and never reassures about money in their own voice. If you
find yourself writing *"and don't worry, you haven't been charged"* as a
Curator line, it belongs in the safety strip instead.

## 3 · THE HARD RULE: ONLY SAY WHAT THE MACHINE KNOWS

This is the rule that prevents the copy from embarrassing itself at scale,
and the current draft breaks it.

The Curator's line when a photograph is accepted currently reads:

> *The light across her hair is doing something lovely — that will carry into
> almost anything.*

That is said over **every** photograph. A man. A dark kitchen snapshot. A
child. Someone's late father. The machine has made no such observation and
cannot; nothing in the analyze response knows about light on hair.

So: **the Curator may only observe what the response actually contains.**

Today that is: a quality verdict, a face size in pixels, body coverage, a
sharpness and brightness reading, and a face count. Nothing about hair,
expression, mood, weather, who the person is, or what they mean to anyone.

Write to what is known, or write about the *work* rather than the
photograph — *"So: what kind of effect are you thinking about today?"* is
safe over any image because it makes no claim.

If a line would be wrong over a photograph of somebody's dead mother, it is
the wrong line.

---

## 4 · THE FAULT LINES MUST AGREE WITH EACH OTHER

There are four photograph faults — face small, soft, dim, unusable — and each
already has its own card copy from r02. The Curator letter beside them
currently says the same thing for all four:

> *That one is a little soft for me.*

So on a face-too-small rejection the letter says "soft" while the card two
inches away says the face sits small. They contradict, and the customer reads
both.

**Either the letter matches the fault, or the letter stays silent and the
card speaks.** One voice at a time. Four matched letters is the better
answer; silence is the acceptable one.

**Never blame the person.** *"This photograph is fighting me."* Not "you
uploaded a bad photo." The photograph is the subject of the sentence, always.

---

## 5 · THE STUDIO IS A REAL PLACE

The existing copy has built a physical workshop and it is worth keeping. It
has a **bench** — *"The rest are still on the bench."* It has **capacity** —
*"The studio's at capacity just now."* Rooms can be **empty** — *"That room is
still in the studio; I have nothing finished to show you in there yet."* And
it can **stumble** — *"The studio stumbled on this one."*

Failures are workshop events, not error codes. A thing went wrong at a bench,
and someone is dealing with it.

---

## 6 · VOCABULARY

**Say:** finishes · pieces · a piece · crafted · the studio · the bench ·
a sitting · your photograph · rooms

**Never say:**

| banned | why | instead |
|---|---|---|
| sculpt, sculpted, sculpture | implies a physical object arrives | craft, crafted |
| render (as a verb or noun) | production language | craft, piece |
| queue (to the customer) | production language | *"the pieces you've chosen"* |
| off, save, discount, deal | not that kind of shop | state the price |
| in-situ / in situ | jargon | in environment |
| image, file, upload (for the output) | it's a piece | piece, work |
| AI, model, prompt, generate | never surfaces | the studio, crafting |

**Crafted Images** takes capitals when it is the product term. The action verb
is **Craft**.

## 7 · MECHANICS

**Signature.** Every Curator letter ends `— C.` with a thin space before it.
Nothing else is signed: not buttons, not the safety strip, not counts.

**British spelling**, consistently. *recognise, favourite, honoured,
colour.* The existing corpus is already British throughout.

**The em-dash is the house pause.** *"nothing is lost"*, *"so I won't send
it"*, *"they always do."* Used for the turn in a sentence. Commas do the
ordinary work.

**Length: two sentences.** Twenty-five to forty words. The intake cards run
slightly longer because they carry a fault and a remedy. Nothing anywhere is
a paragraph.

**Rhythm: one short, one long.** *"A smile. It softens the whole piece, and
the eyes go with it — they always do."* The short sentence names the thing,
the long one earns it.

**No stacked hedges.** Not *"this might possibly be a little soft."* One
qualifier at most: *"a little soft"* is right.

**No exclamation marks. No emoji. No questions the customer can't answer.**
*"What kind of effect are you thinking about today?"* is fine — there is a
floor of rooms right there. *"Isn't that lovely?"* is not.

---

## 8 · WHAT IS BEING SOLD, AND HOW IT IS SOLD

The customer is spending real money on something made from a photograph they
care about. The copy earns that by being **specific and restrained**, never by
being enthusiastic.

Selling happens by describing the work truthfully and letting the customer
want it. *"Weight, grain, and the honesty of real material."* That is the
Earth & Ore line and it is doing the whole job in nine words.

**Never oversell the outcome.** No "stunning", "breathtaking", "you'll love
it". The Curator can say what a finish does; they cannot promise how the
customer will feel.

**Scarcity and urgency are out.** No countdowns, no "only today", no nudging.
The one time-pressure line that exists is honest — *"Ten is the most I can
craft in one go"* — because it is a real limit, not a lever.

---

## 9 · TESTS BEFORE ANY LINE SHIPS

1. Would this be wrong, tasteless, or absurd over a photograph of somebody's
   dead parent? → rewrite.
2. Does it claim something the response does not contain? → rewrite (§3).
3. Does it say something a card next to it contradicts? → rewrite (§4).
4. Does it mention money in the Curator's voice? → move to the safety strip
   (§2).
5. Does it blame the customer? → make the photograph the subject (§4).
6. Two sentences? Signed? British? → fix.
7. Does it contain a banned word (§6)? → the build gate will reject it
   anyway; fix it first.

## 10 · WHAT THIS DOCUMENT DOES NOT COVER

The Concierge is a different speaker and needs its own rules. It handles
disputes, refunds, and re-crafts — moments where money and disappointment are
both live. Three decisions on the launch board are still open there (3.1, 3.2,
3.3), and until they are ruled, its voice cannot be settled.

The provisional position: **the Concierge is the studio's second register
made whole** — plain, factual, unsigned, no charm. When something has gone
wrong with money, charm reads as evasion.
