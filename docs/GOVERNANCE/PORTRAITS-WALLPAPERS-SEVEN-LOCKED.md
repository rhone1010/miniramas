## WHAT THESE SEVEN ESTABLISHED

Every one of them landed on the first or second shot, and the reasons are
worth carrying to the last seven and to the Halloween room.

**Thighs to head.** Not a bust, not a full standing figure. A bust leaves the
bottom half of a tall frame empty; a full figure makes the face too small to
recognise at arm's length. Every body says it in the opening sentence.

**Legs cut off at image bottom.** Rich's addition on Petal Sculpture. It is
what stops the render composing as a photographed object on a plinth and
makes it fill the screen instead.

**The top third is occupied, not empty.** The first neon test came back with
clean sky and it was dead. Rich's fix was dim accent work — thinner tubes
running up and around the top third, dimmed. Every body since names something
quiet up there: leaded flowers, blossom branches, hanging clocks, a space
elevator. The clock still reads; the space is not wasted.

**No lettering, no numerals, no text.** NB2 drew its own phone clock on the
first neon shot and got the date wrong — "Trnday, Nep 26". The ban fixed it
and has held on every render since.

**Bust clauses had to go.** The Portraits bodies end with "no held objects"
and "an arm or hand appears only when it is touching the body". Both are
rules for a bust and both fight a standing figure with hands.

**do not accentuate body parts.** Added on Clockwork after a render
exaggerated the figure. Carried to Balloon. Worth adding to any effect that
rebuilds the whole body in a material.

## STILL OPEN

**The likeness lever has three different scales in play.** Halloween bodies
use `keep 60% likeness`, `likeness should be at least 80%`, and plain
`likeness unmistakable`. Settle on one before either room ships, so effects
are tuned against each other rather than each in isolation.

**Victorian and Renaissance are gendered.** `portraits-bodies.ts` ships a
separate `_woman` body for each. A wallpaper has one subject so it resolves
the same way Portraits does, but both `_woman` bodies still need porting.
